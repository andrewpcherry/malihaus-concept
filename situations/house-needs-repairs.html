What this actually looks like.

In South Florida the thing that most often ends a sale is the roof, because the insurer will not write a policy and without insurance the lender will not fund. Then there is the four point inspection, the wind mitigation report, aluminium wiring, cast iron pipe, unpermitted work somebody did in 1998 and an open claim nobody wants to inherit.

None of that stops us. It changes the number, and it should, but it does not stop us and it does not send you looking for a contractor before you can sell.

The most useful thing you can do is tell us what is actually wrong. A number that is quoted honestly and then held is worth more than a higher number that gets cut the week before closing.

Questions people in this situation actually ask.

The roof is at the end of its life and we cannot get it insured.

That is the single most common reason a South Florida sale collapses, and it is not a reason we walk away. Tell us the age of the roof early so the number we give you is the number that holds.

Is there any repair you would need me to do first?

No. That is the point. No repairs, no clean up, no making it presentable, no bringing anything up to code before we will look at it.

There is unpermitted work on the property.

Very common here, and it does not end the conversation. It is a factor in the price rather than a wall. Tell us about it rather than hoping it does not come up.

What about mould, or damage from a storm?

Both are things we buy. If there is an open insurance claim, say so early, because who keeps the claim proceeds is worth agreeing at the start rather than at the end.

Will you send someone to look at it?

Yes. Somebody sees the house before we commit to a number, which is exactly why the number does not move afterwards.
